# Topics Guide

## What This Section Is

`Topics` stores reusable hierarchical groups in the `categories` collection. Topics organize Articles, Reviews, and News & Events into durable areas such as `Admissions`, `Academics`, `Campus Life`, and `Research`.

## When To Use It

Use `Topics` when content should belong to a stable topic area that visitors may browse repeatedly over time.

Do not use it for:

- one-off keywords that are better handled by `Tags`
- navigation labels that belong in `Navigation Links`, `Headers`, or `Footers`

## How It Connects To The Website

Topics affect how `Articles`, `Reviews`, and `News & Events` are grouped, filtered, and cross-linked. Articles and News & Events support a primary Topic plus optional supporting Topics; Reviews use a primary Topic.

If categories are named poorly or duplicated, the frontend content model becomes messy and related content becomes harder to trust.

## Step-By-Step Medical College Example

Create an `Admissions` category for North Valley Medical College:

1. Open `Topics` in `Taxonomy`.
2. Click `Create New`.
3. Enter the title `Admissions`.
4. Confirm the slug is `admissions`.
5. Leave the parent empty unless you are intentionally nesting it under a larger section.
6. Add a short description explaining that this category covers entry requirements, deadlines, and application guidance.
7. Fill in SEO details if the category will be exposed as an archive or landing page.
8. Save the category.

Expected website result:

- editors can assign `Admissions` as the primary or supporting category on articles and reviews
- related content around admissions becomes more consistent

## Best Practices

- keep category names broad and durable
- use parent categories only when the hierarchy is genuinely useful
- maintain one clear meaning per category
- review category duplication before creating a new one

## Common Mistakes And Recovery

- using categories for temporary campaigns
- creating near-duplicate categories like `Admission` and `Admissions`
- over-nesting categories without a real archive strategy

If editorial grouping becomes confusing, merge usage into the stronger category and stop assigning the weaker one going forward.
